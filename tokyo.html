Tokyo Travel Guide

Essential tips & details for navigating Japan's cyberpunk metropolis

🌐 Arrival & Logistics

  • Airports: You will fly into either Narita (NRT) (further away, take the Skyliner or Narita Express) or Haneda (HND) (much closer to the city center).
  • Connectivity: Pre-order an eSIM or pick up a pocket Wi-Fi at the airport. Google Maps is essential for navigating the train system.
  • Visas: Tourists from many countries (US, EU, UK, Canada) get a 90-day visa-free entry stamp on arrival.

💴 Cash & Payments

  • Cash is King: While credit cards are widely accepted now, many small ramen shops, temples, and vending machines still only accept cash. Keep Yen on you!
  • IC Transit Cards: Get a digital Suica or Pasmo card added to your Apple/Google Wallet. You can tap it for trains, buses, convenience stores, and vending machines.
  • ATMs: The easiest place to withdraw cash using international debit cards is at any 7-Eleven (7-Bank) or FamilyMart.

🚆 Navigating the Trains

  • Subway vs. JR: Tokyo's train lines are run by different companies (Tokyo Metro and JR East). The Yamanote Line (JR) is a massive loop connecting major hubs like Shibuya, Shinjuku, and Tokyo Station.
  • Train Etiquette: Keep your voice to a whisper, do not talk on your phone, and put your backpack in front of you on crowded cars. No eating or drinking on local commuter trains!

🇯🇵 Cultural Etiquette

  • No Tipping: Tipping is non-existent and can actually be seen as confusing or insulting. Excellent service is already included!
  • Trash: There are almost no public trash cans on the street. Carry a small plastic bag and hold onto your trash until you return to your hotel or spot a bin next to a vending machine (only for bottles/cans).
  • Walking & Eating: Avoid eating or drinking while walking down the street. Consume it next to the shop or vending machine where you bought it.

🏙️ Neighborhood Guide

  • Shibuya & Shinjuku: Neon lights, nightlife, massive shopping centers, and the famous Shibuya Crossing.
  • Akihabara: The anime, manga, gaming, and electronics capital of the world.
  • Asakusa: Home to Senso-ji, Tokyo's oldest and most iconic Buddhist temple, offering a traditional historic vibe.
  • Harajuku: Pop culture, quirky street fashion on Takeshita Street, and great street food (crepes!).

📱 Must-Download Apps

  • Google Maps: Incredibly accurate for Tokyo's complex train platforms and exit numbers.
  • Google Translate: Use the live camera feature to read menus, food labels, and signs instantly.
  • Japan Travel by NAVITIME: Excellent alternative for route planning, especially if trying to filter routes covered by transit passes.
